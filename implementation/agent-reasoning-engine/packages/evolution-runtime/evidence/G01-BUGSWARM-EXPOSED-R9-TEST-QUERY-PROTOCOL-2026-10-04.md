# G01 exposed r9 — failed-test query channel acquisition screen

Status: prospectively fixed before r9 source-only rankings or evaluation. All 24 r8 repositories and labels are now exposed development data; this screen cannot confirm superiority. Its hypothesis is that the full-log-tail query obscures useful failed-test symbols, as suggested by external query-type-aware fault-localization research ([LLmiRQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03754)).

Use the unchanged ARE BM25 file ranker over each exact r5 failed-commit code root, but submit only the `failed tests:` field extracted from the sealed r6 query, excluding the `log tail:` field. A case without failed-test names yields no channel candidates and retains its r8 list. Preserve the complete channel ranking and its source-tree/query identity before evaluator access. This is a separate acquisition channel, not a replacement of the sealed ARE comparator.

For each case, begin with the r8 graph-r2/test-edge-r3 top-30 ordered union. Append up to 20 *new non-test* source paths from the failed-test-channel ranking in order. Then append the r8 stratified additions in their original order until the 80-path cap. Keep all union paths and use no labels, patch data, passing-commit code, or scorer outputs in selection. The channel may displace lower-priority old stratified additions when the 80-path cap binds; do not revise that priority after scoring. Seal all 24 candidate lists before looking at r8 gold labels for evaluation.

Evaluator-only exposed screen: compare old r8 candidate reach with new r9 candidate reach across all 24 direct-tree gold sets, report newly reached and lost cases, maximum/total candidate counts, and channel-index elapsed time. Advancement to a separately frozen 8K GLM packet study requires complete 24/24 source identities, candidate reach at least 20/24, zero old-reach losses, at most 80 unique paths per case, and no label leakage. A failure ends this r9 hypothesis; subsequent ideas require separately versioned protocols. No r9 result is a fresh claim.
