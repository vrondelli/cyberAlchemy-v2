Executive overview

The continuity problem

Teams usually lose intent not in one dramatic failure, but through a sequence of plausible translations: requirement to architecture, architecture to code, code to tests, and tests to claims.

Meaning drifts

The same word acquires different meanings across product, engineering, and formal models.

Evidence inflates

A passing test, solver result, or proof is allowed to imply more than its exact model and observation justify.

Authority blurs

Generated artifacts and technical checks are treated as if they could approve meaning, implementation, or release.

CyberAlchemy is an attempt to preserve an inspectable chain from the words people use to the bounded conclusions engineers may honestly make.

Stages 1–3

Definitions and business ontology

Formal precision cannot repair a term whose owner, scope, or intended use was never settled.

Definition Pack

The Define process starts from a real business question and records the minimum semantic context required for downstream work:

What is being attempted?

Goal, original intent, bounded context, intended consumers, constraints, and desired outcome.

What must be understood?

Concepts, exact sources, supporting evidence, meaning owners, and unresolved questions.

Every concept receives a visible disposition

DispositionMeaningWhy it matters
ReuseAn existing owned definition fits this context.Prevents needless synonyms.
SpecializeAn existing definition fits after narrowing its context.Makes contextual differences explicit.
ProposeA new candidate definition is required.Separates invention from accepted meaning.
OpenThe answer is not ready.Prevents ambiguity from becoming an implementation guess.

From vocabulary to ontology

A business ontology is not merely a collection of nouns. It is a governed meaning layer:

Each important ontology record can carry provenance, branch or role, confidence, candidate or promoted state, drift handling, and downstream-use obligations. Business, system, and bridge branches remain separate so that implementation evidence cannot silently redefine business intent.

Non-collapse rule. An ontology is a navigable and governed map of meaning. Its existence does not make every node canonical, prove a system exists, or authorize implementation.

Stage 4

FormalClaim: a precise bridge, not a proof

A FormalClaim freezes the meaning-bearing inputs of one proposition while keeping evaluation context, provider syntax, and evidence outside the claim’s identity.

FormalClaim = ( business meaning, explicit accepted-definition closure, formal signature, canonical proposition, meaning-bearing language profile, assumptions and scope, claim ceiling, deterministic identity )

Example: order submission eligibility

Business voice

“An order may be submitted only if it has at least one line and its total is non-negative.”

Formal voice

∀ o : Order · HasLine(o) ∧ HasNonNegativeTotal(o)

Domain-context voice

This proposition governs submission eligibility. It does not claim that an order was persisted, a message was delivered, payment was authorized, or a production system is correct.

One claim, several formal routes

ProfileQuestion it can addressBound that remains
FOL / sorted FOLPrecise predicates over an explicit signatureA language, not an executor
SMTSatisfiability or validity of an admitted projectionUnknown and timeout retain their native meaning
TLA+/TLCTemporal behavior across a declared state machineModel checking is bounded by model and explored state space
AlloyRelational instances and counterexamplesBounded by the exact scope
LeanTheorem checking in an exact formal environmentDoes not show the running implementation preserves the model
SouffléFinite closed-world derivationDepends on exact facts and rules
Human explainability. The system does not “translate a formula back into truth.” It creates an understanding view made of source-linked ontology nodes—BusinessMeaning, definition binding, signature, proposition, assumptions, ceiling, dependencies, projections, and evidence. Each node explains one part while the original FormalClaim retains semantic identity.

The understanding layer

Store as a graph. Explain as a sequence.

DomainSpec preserves exact claim structure as a graph, but presents the explanation in the order a person can follow: context, formal source, operator, operands, definitions, recomposition, and boundary.

Source graph

Reusable rigor

What is proved once—and what must be re-established

Generated code does not begin from an unexamined template. It begins from admitted, versioned realization contracts—but reusable evidence must not be confused with proof of a new application.

The reusable construction basis

Formalized roles

Each meta-type has machine-checkable formation criteria and typed edge-participation rules. The active backend language contains 13 roles and 31 governed relationship signatures.

Admitted realizations

Each active role has one selected TypeScript, Python, and Rust realization: 39 exact, digest-bound realization rows exercised by deterministic generation and bounded static conformance.

Graph-bound proof

For an exact compiled project, DomainSpec can generate a Lean proof unit covering applicable DS-D8 graph obligations and selected L1 structural facts, then check the resulting certificate independently.

What the generated project can inherit

Reusable evidenceWhat it can supportWhat it cannot establish

The defensible advantage. A generated project starts from a pre-qualified, content-bound construction basis. Applicable structural obligations can be discharged by fresh replay of the reusable proof and conformance machinery instead of being rediscovered manually.
Not an automatic EVF pass. EVF may count only evidence bound to the new project’s exact source, graph, realization rows, generated bytes, assumptions, provider, environment, and obligation IDs. New composition, integration, runtime, and business obligations still require their own current evidence. The result, when the required join closes, is PASS_BOUNDED for named obligations—not “all generated code is proven.”

Stages 6–7

Compilation, system ontology, and realization

DomainSpec compilation answers a concrete question: “Given this exact authored model, what system structure follows from it, what must a realization provide, and how can we identify the result?”

Compilation in plain language

Input

Exact AlchLang and DomainSpec declarations: typed concepts, allowed relationships, FormalClaim bindings, integration intent, and profile selections.

Work

Resolve names, reject missing or invalid endpoints, check type–relationship compatibility, select exact realization contracts, and derive required obligations.

Output

A content-identified compiled graph, readable projections, system ontology, implementation plan or artifacts, and receipts binding all outputs to their inputs.

A concrete compiler example

Authored source: Rule OrderCanBeSubmitted --enforces--> Operation SubmitOrder Operation SubmitOrder --produces--> Event OrderSubmitted DomainSpec checks: • both referenced nodes exist • “enforces” accepts Rule → Operation • “produces” accepts Operation → Event • the selected target has realizations for all three roles Compiler products: • graph: exact nodes and typed edges • obligation: SubmitOrder must be guarded by OrderCanBeSubmitted • system ontology: trace from business nodes to system roles and residue • receipt: hashes of source, profile, compiler, graph, and generated output

The compiled graph is the machine’s resolved model. The system ontology is the inspectable traceability projection of that model. Generated code is one selected realization. None of these is allowed to contradict or independently redefine the authored source.

Realization remains explicit

A runtime profile separately selects a target such as TypeScript, Python, or Rust; a provider pack; capability-to-provider mappings; and symbolic configuration or secret slots. The domain model does not change when PostgreSQL is exchanged for another compatible persistence provider. Runtime observations also stay separate: application commit, provider acceptance, delivery, consumer commit, and business completion do not imply one another.

Stages 8–9

TDE and formal providers

DomainSpec identifies a requirement. TDE turns a supported requirement into neutral, reproducible test obligations. A provider executes one concrete version of those obligations.

The three transformations

1
DomainSpec obligation: “The SubmitOrder realization must apply OrderCanBeSubmitted before the state-changing commit.”
what must hold
2
TDE derivation: generate neutral cases such as an empty order being rejected, a valid order being admitted, and no rejected case producing a commit or event.
what to check
3
Provider execution: translate those neutral cases into an exact TypeScript test, Python test, Rust test, solver query, or other admitted provider request.
what happened

Test Derivation Engine boundary

current FormalClaim + capability assessment + preservation-backed executable projection → provider-neutral obligations and bounded cases

A neutral obligation carries stable identities, normalized inputs, expected relations or oracle requirements, provenance, residue, and a claim ceiling. It deliberately contains no TypeScript, Python, Rust, Lean, SMT, Alloy, TLC, or Soufflé branch. This lets the same obligation be realized by more than one provider without turning provider syntax into business meaning.

Classification before execution

executable-bounded solver-admissible proof-provider-required unsupported indeterminate

Only an admitted executable-bounded slice enters ordinary test derivation. The theorem route, solver route, temporal model-checking route, relational route, and Datalog route remain distinct.

Provider evidence is exact

A provider-runtime binding identifies the provider version and digest, source artifact, command, environment allowlist, fixtures, resource limits, network policy, timeout, and supported result meanings. Raw output remains reachable.

TLA+/TLC route

A FormalClaim with temporal semantics is projected into a declared state model and properties. TLC can report behavior within the explored state space. It cannot establish correctness outside that model or show that implementation code preserves the model.

Lean route

An exact theorem statement and environment are checked through a theorem-provider request. A checked theorem is evidence about that model and binding—not proof of runtime correspondence.

Executable route

TDE derives cases; a selected runtime executes them; the result is bounded by the code, fixture, environment, and observation boundary.

Cross-provider discipline

Provider agreement is non-voting. Several weak results cannot vote themselves into a stronger claim; disagreement, unknown, missing, or stale evidence remains visible.

Fail-closed rule. Unsupported, timeout, malformed, stale, unknown, provider error, and missing prerequisites never become PASS through aggregation.

Stage 10

EVF: the evidence accountant

The Evolvable Validation Framework asks whether every declared path received the right kind of current evidence—and exposes exactly what remains unresolved.

For each reachable path p: desired residue ρ(p) exists owner(p) exists obligations O(p) are explicit required evidence kinds K(p) are explicit current evidence E(p) is digest-bound supported(p) only if every obligation and required evidence kind has current positive evidence, comparable under a non-inflating join, with no contradiction, stale input, structural defect, or authority defect.

Evidence kinds are orthogonal

Evidence kindAnswersDoes not establish alone
Specification closureDid we describe the declared paths and obligations?Implementation or behavior
Formal proofDoes a proposition follow from an exact model?Implementation preservation
Bounded explorationDoes a property hold in a finite declared state space?Behavior outside the bound
Implementation testWhat did code do for named inputs and environment?Exhaustive or live-system behavior
Runtime observationWhat happened in one named execution?Universal or future correctness
Operational controlDid the system stop, retain, reconcile, alert, or escalate?Internal code correctness

Typed outcomes

PASS_BOUNDED NON_PASSUNSUPPORTEDINDETERMINATE STALEMISSINGFAILEDBLOCKED

A positive conclusion is bound to the exact claim, projection, assumptions, provider, environment, inputs, result semantics, and source identities named by the join. EVF produces candidate-readiness evidence; it does not approve the product.

Hype scales code. Rigor scales trust. The scarce resource is not output—it is justified confidence.

D65: evidence does not authorize itself

The invariant

asserted_strength(claim) ≤ strength(declared_support)

Support may be a source citation, validation, witness, accepted premise, owner decision, bounded model-checking result, or formal proof, depending on the claim. The rule does not demand certainty; it demands honest wording.

Present implementation posture

IMPLEMENTEDCanonical 13-type backend language; typed relationship checks; deterministic graph compilation; manifests and receipts; selected TypeScript, Python, and Rust realization evidence; substantial CLI, lowering, and provider-generation paths.
PARTIALAlchLang authoring-to-runtime composition; FormalClaim contracts and profile model; runtime/provider paths; system-ontology projections; bounded TDE/EVF candidate verticals and independent replay controls.
OPENOne reconciled FormalClaim source contract across current TDE intake; the general TDE/formal-provider/EVF join; complete concise AlchLang surface; full production correspondence; external publication, release, and deployment decisions.

What the architecture claims

Yes

It is possible to preserve exact identities, provenance, typed relations, obligations, observations, and claim ceilings across a long software-construction chain.

No

The existence of that chain does not prove the business is right, every translation is complete, the implementation preserves every model, or a product should be released.

Discussion invitation

Where formal-methods criticism is most valuable

The goal of this document is not to present a closed system. It is to make the seams precise enough to attack.

Semantic preservation

What evidence is sufficient to show that a projection preserves the business meaning selected by a FormalClaim?

Temporal adequacy

How should the system decide that a workflow claim genuinely requires a TLA+ route, rather than an executable or relational approximation?

Model–implementation correspondence

What is the strongest defensible bridge between a checked temporal model and an observed implementation?

Evidence joins

Which results are comparable, and what algebra prevents several weak observations from inflating into a stronger claim?

Change and invalidation

Which changes create a new FormalClaim, which only invalidate a projection, and which make prior evidence stale?

Human review

Can formal, plain-language, and domain-context views improve comprehension without becoming competing semantic authorities?

The proposed bridge is not “business prose → automatic proof.” It is “owned meaning → explicit formal subject → bounded providers → non-inflating evidence → separate decision.”
