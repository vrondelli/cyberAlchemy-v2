01 · Problem

Generation is cheap. Accountable use is not.

AI can produce code, analyses, documents, and decisions faster than many teams can review their sources, limits, ownership, and readiness for action.

Can we use this metric or AI-assisted workflow output for this business action, under this scope, with this owner?
  • Where did the result come from?
  • What could invalidate it?
  • Which checks were run?
  • Who owns the decision?
  • What does a pass not mean?

BI & analytics

A disputed metric, unstable definition, or source chain blocks an operating decision.

Operations

A workflow crosses teams and tools without a reviewable evidence or approval trail.

Product & engineering

AI-assisted work moves faster than ownership, review, and continuation practices.

Sensitive workflows

Confidential artifacts need explicit custody, approval, retention, and reuse boundaries.

02 · Customer stories

Three environments. One accountable decision pattern.

CyberAlchemy does not begin by replacing the customer's systems. It enters at the decision boundary, connects existing evidence, makes uncertainty visible, preserves human authority, and leaves a receipt the next cycle can reuse.

01 Live premises

Thursday afternoon before a busy weekend

A multi-site hospitality and venue operator must decide how to staff, stock, and promote each location. POS, bookings, schedules, inventory exports, campaign reports, and local spreadsheets all exist, but they disagree on time windows, cancellations, and what success means. Managers are debating the dashboard instead of making the operating decision.

How should each site staff, stock, and act for the next operating window, given what the evidence actually supports?

CyberAlchemy maps source ownership and definitions, exposes denominator and confound problems, runs named freshness and consistency checks, and routes the result to the regional operations owner. The customer keeps its systems and authority.

Integration map
POS Bookings Scheduling Inventory Campaigns
  1. Bind one operating window and accountable owner.
  2. Reconcile definitions, denominators, freshness, and confounds.
  3. Issue pass / flag / block inputs for the decision.
  4. Record the action and what to measure afterward.
Output: Metric Trust decision receipt
02 Technology company

Release day after AI-assisted development

A software company is preparing a billing release. AI systems contributed code, tests, migration notes, and analysis. The evidence is spread across pull requests, issue tickets, CI, security scans, feature flags, reviewer comments, and incident history. The release owner cannot tell whether every obligation implied by the release intent is covered.

Do we release, narrow, pause, or roll back, and who owns the remaining risk?

CyberAlchemy translates release intent into explicit obligations, joins existing evidence to each obligation, keeps missing proof visible, and records human exceptions and stop conditions. Formal witnesses attach only to named properties, never to the release as a whole.

Integration map
Git Issues CI / tests Security Observability
  1. Bind release intent, owner, scope, and rollback boundary.
  2. Derive obligations and link current evidence.
  3. Expose passed, failed, flagged, and missing obligations.
  4. Record owner verdict, exceptions, and next-release residue.
Output: AI work / release readiness receipt
03 Confidential workflow

A sensitive document waiting for approval

A legal, financial, healthcare, or internal-risk team uses AI to prepare a document from sensitive sources. The draft may be useful, but the reviewer cannot easily see which sources were used, whether required sections were checked, who held custody, which version was approved, or what must be retained.

Is this specific output ready for the accountable reviewer to approve, revise, reject, or retain?

CyberAlchemy integrates identity, role, document/version, approval, retention, and safely capturable model-use records. Digests and redacted metadata can preserve traceability without centralizing confidential content. The owner remains the final authority.

Integration map
Identity Case / document Versions Approvals Retention
  1. Bind custody, reviewer authority, purpose, and exclusions.
  2. Connect source use and checks through safe metadata.
  3. Gate approval on explicit evidence and exceptions.
  4. Record decision, retention, privacy boundary, and non-claims.
Output: Confidential-workflow readiness receipt

These are illustrative composite scenarios, not customer deployments, traction, or compliance claims. They show how one governed integration pattern changes with the customer's evidence and risk environment.

05 · Market

Start with a decision condition, not a fictional TAM.

A qualified opportunity has a named owner, a consequential blocked action, accessible evidence, a budget path, and a fixed receipt-shaped scope.

Founder-led market entry

  • Interview: Find one number or workflow the buyer cannot confidently use.
  • Qualify: Identify owner, urgency, workaround, access, and budget path.
  • Sell: Paid diagnostic or fixed sprint with acceptance and stop rules.
  • Deliver: Evidence, readiness verdict, owner decision, and receipt.
  • Expand or stop: Test second workflow, retainer, or no further work.

Competitive categories

  • BI, data quality, lineage, and observability
  • AI governance and risk platforms
  • Consulting and systems integration
  • Requirements, testing, and traceability tools
  • Agent frameworks and coding assistants
  • Formal-methods and correctness specialists

CyberAlchemy's intended distinction is the joined delivery unit: source-linked obligations, accountable owner authority, explicit gates, receipts, continuation, and bounded formal or deterministic checks where justified. Buyer preference and competitive advantage remain unvalidated. Kodamai is one relevant narrow comparator, not the only competitor.

07 · Roadmap

The next 90 days decide the business shape.

Each stage has a promotion signal and a fail signal. The plan is designed to strengthen, narrow, or stop based on evidence.

Days 1-30

Validate the problem

  • 5-10 buyer interviews
  • Fixed SOW specimen
  • One bounded readiness specimen
  • Fresh technical receipts
  • Founder-complete financial inputs
Fail: interest stays abstract, ownerless, or without a budget path.
Days 31-60

Sell and deliver

  • One signed paid SOW
  • Invoice and collection evidence
  • Measured hours and direct costs
  • Accepted receipt bundle
  • Named owner verdict
Fail: the work is unpaid, unscoped, uncollected, or has no owner decision.
Days 61-90

Test repeatability

  • Up to three paid pilots
  • Same core receipt bundle
  • Reuse and adaptation log
  • Labor and cycle-time comparison
  • Second workflow or retainer signal
Fail: no reusable core, no delivery leverage, or no buyer continuation.

Market risk

The problem may not be urgent or budgeted. The gate is repeated owner, urgency, and willingness-to-pay evidence.

Delivery risk

The work may remain bespoke. The gate is reuse across three paid cases with stable quality and lower variance.

Capacity risk

Two founders must sell, deliver, research, and operate. Parallel work is earned through measured templates and quality.

Claim risk

Overclaiming would contradict the product thesis. Every external claim keeps its evidence class and demotion gate.

Explicit non-claims

  • No finished or deployment-proven platform
  • No product-market fit or validated platform revenue
  • No certification, compliance, or auditor approval
  • No formally verified platform or provably correct agents
  • No claim that receipts prove truth
  • No proven moat, unique, first, or only category claim
  • No implemented Evidence Exchange or public verifier
  • No production legal SaaS or confidential-data guarantee
  • No live ad integration or shipping IDE plugin
  • No public-ready DomainSpec v2 or CAV2
Investment thesis

From intent to evidence to accountable action.

CyberAlchemy has assembled a broad substrate across typed specification, governance, ledgered execution, formal methods, product surfaces, vertical cases, and research. The decisive business question is whether that substrate can repeatedly solve one paid buyer problem.

Fund the production of the next decisive witnesses: buyer language, a paid SOW, collected cash, an accepted receipt, measured delivery economics, reusable components, and demand for the next workflow.
