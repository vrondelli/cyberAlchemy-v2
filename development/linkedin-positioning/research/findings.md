## Findings

### F1 — The public category is governed human–AI work, not autonomous AI

- **Claim:** CyberAlchemy can credibly open with governed workflows shared by people and AI systems, provided people remain responsible for decisions and for accepting changes.
- **Evidence:** [research.md §Agent 2 — Ashby, W. Ross](./research.md#agent-2--ashby-w-ross--cybernetic-governance)
- **Implication:** The copy should describe AI-assisted workflows as able to surface evidence, run checks, and propose changes, while reserving judgment and acceptance for people.

### F2 — The audience and outputs must be concrete immediately

- **Claim:** The primary audience is teams coordinating complex AI-assisted knowledge work, and the most legible examples are decision maps, bounded experiments, source-backed guides or briefs, and reviewable artifacts.
- **Evidence:** [research.md §Agent 3 — Simon, Herbert](./research.md#agent-3--simon-herbert--audience-translation)
- **Implication:** The company description should name the audience in its opening sentence and use recognizable outputs before introducing intellectual foundations.

### F3 — Formal claims must stay attached to particular constructions

- **Claim:** The repository supports a narrow statement that category-theoretic models and Lean-checked constructions test particular claims about refinement and information loss; it does not support whole-system formal-verification language.
- **Evidence:** [research.md §Agent 1 — Lawvere, F. William](./research.md#agent-1--lawvere-f-william--formal-reconstruction)
- **Implication:** Category theory and Lean should appear as claim-bounding research instruments, never as guarantees of the entire method, organization, or software system.

### F4 — Company and founder require different voices

- **Claim:** The company page should speak institutionally and offer-first, while the founder entry should explain personal contribution without collapsing collaborative work into solo authorship.
- **Evidence:** [research.md §Agent 3 — Simon, Herbert](./research.md#agent-3--simon-herbert--audience-translation)
- **Implication:** The company owns the category proposition; the founder names the framing, research, governance, and workflow-design responsibilities they lead.

### F5 — The credible maturity claim is compact and developmental

- **Claim:** Explicit authority separation, review surfaces, and evidence discipline are defensible, but the full operational loop, adoption outcomes, and end-to-end product maturity are not established.
- **Evidence:** [research.md §Agent 2 — Ashby, W. Ross](./research.md#agent-2--ashby-w-ross--cybernetic-governance)
- **Implication:** One brief maturity sentence is enough: the work is developing through bounded applications and research, with no claims about scale, universal deployment, or autonomous operation.

## Analysis

### T1 — Formal credibility versus whole-system proof inflation

- **Formal basis:** Specific Lean constructions model residue under schema refinement and establish particular witnesses concerning gluing and information loss.
- **Public risk:** Readers may interpret “Lean” or “formal” as a claim that the entire system is verified.
- **Evidence:** [research.md §Agent 1 — Lawvere, F. William](./research.md#agent-1--lawvere-f-william--formal-reconstruction)
- **Resolution:** Use the exact bounded formulation: “category-theoretic models and Lean-checked constructions test particular claims about refinement and information loss.”

### T2 — Governance language versus public intelligibility

- **Governance basis:** The work separates evidence, proposals, owner decisions, and accepted changes.
- **Audience need:** First-contact readers need recognizable work products, not internal authority architecture.
- **Evidence:** [research.md §Agent 2 — Ashby, W. Ross](./research.md#agent-2--ashby-w-ross--cybernetic-governance), [research.md §Agent 3 — Simon, Herbert](./research.md#agent-3--simon-herbert--audience-translation)
- **Resolution:** Explain governance behavior through decision maps, checks, briefs, and reviewable artifacts; omit internal layer names and mechanics.

### T3 — Founder clarity versus authorship inflation

- **Founder requirement:** The profile must communicate clear responsibility and expertise.
- **Collaborative reality:** Methods and artifacts are developed with human teammates through AI-assisted workflows under human review.
- **Evidence:** [research.md §Agent 3 — Simon, Herbert](./research.md#agent-3--simon-herbert--audience-translation), [research.md §Agent 2 — Ashby, W. Ross](./research.md#agent-2--ashby-w-ross--cybernetic-governance)
- **Resolution:** Use contribution verbs such as “lead,” “frame,” “research,” and “design.” Do not describe AI as an author, co-creator, or decision maker.

### Cross-cutting observations

All three readings converge on the same ordering: human problem and audience first, concrete governed work second, formal basis third, and maturity boundary last. The strongest copy is therefore neither a generic governance claim nor a technical abstract. It presents inspectable outputs, names human accountability, and uses formal methods only to bound particular questions.

# Selected LinkedIn Copy

## Company tagline

Governed workflows for people and AI systems doing complex knowledge work

## Company About

CyberAlchemy develops governed workflows shared by people and AI systems for teams coordinating complex AI-assisted knowledge work.

We are developing methods to help teams turn scattered intent, research, and system activity into decision maps, bounded experiments, source-backed guides and briefs, and other reviewable artifacts. The aim is to preserve context, evidence, limits, ownership, and next steps so complex work can continue across people, tools, and time.

Within these workflows, AI may surface evidence, run checks, and propose changes. People remain responsible for decisions and for choosing which changes are accepted.

Our research draws on knowledge governance, systems thinking, formal methods, and software design. Category-theoretic models and Lean-checked constructions test particular claims about refinement and information loss.

CyberAlchemy is a developing method and practice, advanced through bounded applications, collaborative research, and human-reviewed implementation.

Founder | AI Governance Architect & Researcher | Designing governed human–AI workflows for complex knowledge work

Founder | AI Governance Architect & Researcher

## Founder experience description

I lead the framing, research, governance, and system and workflow design behind CyberAlchemy.

My work focuses on how teams can use AI in complex knowledge work without losing human responsibility, source traceability, or continuity. I design workflows that turn research, decisions, experiments, and implementation activity into reviewable artifacts such as decision maps, bounded experiments, and source-backed guides and briefs.

I develop these methods collaboratively with human teammates through AI-assisted workflows under human review. AI may help surface evidence, run checks, and propose changes; people remain responsible for decisions and for determining which changes are accepted.

The research combines knowledge governance, systems thinking, formal methods, and software design. Category-theoretic models and Lean-checked constructions test particular claims about refinement and information loss.

The work is developing through bounded applications and research, with an emphasis on inspectability, human agency, and artifacts that others can review and continue.

Knowledge governance, systems thinking, formal methods, and software design—with category-theoretic models and Lean-checked constructions testing particular claims about refinement and information loss.

## Rejected alternatives

### Alternative B — Formal-first

> Category-theoretic and Lean-grounded infrastructure for verified human–AI systems.

**Reason rejected:** “Verified human–AI systems” improperly extends bounded construction-level evidence to the whole system. Leading with mathematical machinery also delays the audience, problem, and concrete value.

### Alternative A without C — Output-only

> We create decision maps, briefs, and experiments for AI-assisted teams.

**Reason rejected:** Concrete but insufficiently differentiated. It does not communicate governance, human responsibility, continuity, or the conditions under which AI participates.

### Alternative C without A — Agency-first

> Human-centered governance for the future of AI collaboration.

**Reason rejected:** Directionally aligned but generic. It does not show what the work produces or give a skeptical reader evidence that the proposition has operational form.

### Autonomous-system framing

> CyberAlchemy builds self-governing AI systems that make complex decisions safely.

**Reason rejected:** Unsupported and contrary to the required responsibility boundary. AI does not hold decision authority, and no evidence supports autonomous or safety-guarantee claims.
