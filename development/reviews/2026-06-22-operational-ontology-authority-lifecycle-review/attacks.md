## Round 1 Attacks

### Peirce - Fidelity/Governance Lens

Initial attack defended the user's collapse instinct.

Finding:

- `Operational Ontology` is not canonical now. D19 says it remains a
  source-specific candidate branch term pending later decision.
- D23 already owns the lifecycle distinction between kind, status, evidence,
  commitment, provenance, owner, and residue.
- `LIFECYCLE-MODEL.md` performs D23-shaped separation work for ontology use.
- D22 supplies the uniqueness test for whether a new ontology grouping deserves
  admission.

Initial recommendation:

- Collapse or rename the currently evidenced lifecycle content into D23 unless
  a later D22 uniqueness test proves that `Operational Ontology` adds a distinct
  operational distinction.

### Russell - Ownership/Reference-Integrity Lens

Initial attack rejected identity collapse as a category error.

Finding:

- D19 owns typed knowledge objects, typed relations, branch or role assignment,
  evidence links, confidence, promotion state, gates, drift routes, and use
  obligations.
- D23 owns only lifecycle-axis separation and explicitly is not a complete status
  schema or ontology-node rule.
- `LIFECYCLE-MODEL.md` says ontology may represent rails and projections but
  must not fill the verdict hole.
- `RELATION-MODEL.md` requires endpoint, owner, evidence, authority effect, and
  forbidden-collapse fields before a D19-D23 dependency can be relied on.

Initial recommendation:

- Reject `Operational Ontology = D23`.
- Treat `Operational Ontology` as a candidate D19 branch, subtype, or use case
  that may expose D23 lifecycle posture as one projection.

### Wittgenstein - Operability Lens

Initial attack rejected both extremes.

Finding:

- Current authority does not support either `Operational Ontology == D23` or
  `Operational Ontology is fully separate`.
- D19 explicitly leaves the term unresolved under ontology.
- D23 supplies lifecycle-axis separation, but no one axis may silently stand in
  for another.
- The lifecycle model makes projection read-model-only and blocks ontology from
  owning verdicts.

Initial recommendation:

- Keep `Operational Ontology` non-canonical until definitions-governance applies
  a decision test:
  - subtype if it adds operation-specific object or relation obligations;
  - branch label if it is only a D19 view for agents, runs, or actions;
  - alias if it adds no distinction beyond D19;
  - separate term only if it has non-ontology obligations that D19 cannot carry.

## Robot-Talks Convergence

After seeing the opposing attacks, all three reviewers converged on the same
middle shape.

### Peirce Final Position

Revised verdict:

- Reject identity collapse.
- Preserve the governance collapse only for the current lifecycle evidence:
  lifecycle/status/owner/confidence claims are D23-shaped.
- D19 remains the ontology carrier.
- Definitions-governance should decide, using D22's test, whether the term earns
  a distinct D19 branch, subtype, use-case, alias, or retirement.

Final change requests:

- Update handoff wording to make the D22 test explicit.
- Keep D19's candidate boundary, but add the decision test.
- Annotate the lifecycle model as lifecycle-axis separation for ontology use.
- Do not add `Operational Ontology` as a D23 alias unless the phrase is stripped
  of all ontology content.

### Russell Final Position

Revised verdict:

- The phrase may collapse or retire if it adds no D22-unique distinction.
- The stronger identity claim still fails: D23 cannot replace D19's ontology
  carrier.

Final change requests:

- Replace broad identity wording with:
  `Operational Ontology is a source-specific candidate under D19; it may use
  D23 lifecycle axes as posture/projection, but D23 does not supply ontology
  node/relation/evidence semantics.`
- Add a D22 admission test.
- Require any D19-D23 bridge to be represented as a typed candidate relation
  with owner, evidence, effect, and forbidden collapse.

### Wittgenstein Final Position

Revised verdict:

- D22 uniqueness should be the admission test before `Operational Ontology` gets
  any canonical surface.
- If the phrase only means lifecycle posture, status/evidence/owner separation,
  or operational readout, rewrite it into D23 language instead of promoting it.
- If it means an operational branch/view of ontology, it inherits D19.

Final change requests:

- Add an explicit open decision under D19:
  `Operational Ontology classification: D23 rewrite, D19 branch/subtype, D19
  use case with D23 projection, or separate term`.
- Add D22 uniqueness as the admission rule.
- Add a drift-audit follow-up for existing narrative uses.

## Synthesis And Verification

### Synthesis

Noether synthesis:

- `Operational Ontology` remains a D19-carried ontology candidate until
  definitions-governance decides, using CAV2-D22's orthogonal-classification
  test, whether it adds a non-redundant distinction.
- `CAV2-D23` supplies lifecycle-axis separation.
- Ontology may carry authorized read-model projections, but D23 does not own or
  rename the ontology term.

### Verifier

Quine requested wording changes, not a substantive reversal.

Required fixes:

- Replace "D22 decides" with "definitions-governance decides using CAV2-D22's
  orthogonal-classification test."
- Replace "D23 as lifecycle posture projection" with "D23 supplies
  lifecycle-axis separation; ontology may carry authorized read-model
  projections."
- Any D19-D23 bridge relation must be candidate/projection-only and must record
  `owner_route`, `evidence_refs`, `authority_effect`, and `forbidden_collapse`.

Verified verdict:

- `Operational Ontology` remains a D19-carried candidate term.
- D22 is the right admission test for non-redundant distinction.
- D23 can constrain lifecycle posture projections inside ontology, but does not
  own or rename the ontology term.
