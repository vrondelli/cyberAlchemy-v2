## 4. Strongest corrected thesis

Adaptive AI interfaces and literary narratives can both shape attention, affect, interpretation, and sometimes beliefs, but through different mechanisms and agency structures. Narrative effects are generally modest and context-dependent. Adaptive AI may additionally introduce profiling, scale, feedback loops, optimization, and personalized persuasion, producing distinct autonomy and privacy risks.

A defensible Sovereign Biocentric program is therefore not a claim that AI detects inner truth, rewires belief, guarantees flourishing, or permanently owns human cognition. It is a testable human-centered governance proposal:

- Minimize sensitive data and process it locally where feasible.
- Prohibit covert manipulation, punitive monitoring, and consequential mental-state inference.
- Preserve meaningful inspect, correct, refuse, revoke, delete, disable, and appeal rights.
- Expose objectives, uncertainty, evidence, and responsible actors.
- Evaluate bounded productivity, well-being, creativity, diversity, and equity outcomes with preregistered positive and negative controls.
- Never promote identity, provenance, or a cryptographic signature into truth, authorization, ownership, or authority without independent empirical, policy, and legal gates.

## 5. Counterexamples and limiting cases

- A fixed literary text is not adaptive and does not collect biometric data. Readers can resist, reinterpret, criticize, and contextualize it.
- Fiction reading may have a small positive effect on social cognition [S06], contradicting a uniformly manipulative model.
- Wordsworth emphasizes reflective recollection [S07], while Shelley frames imagination as a route to sympathy [S08].
- Assistive AI can improve bounded writing and customer-support performance [S12–S13]; AI is not only a manipulation engine.
- Generative AI can improve individual creative output while homogenizing collective output [S14].
- User preferences, external referrals, and the wider information ecosystem partly explain extreme-content consumption [S11].
- Protected incubation can aid a specific creative task [S16]; trauma and overload should not be romanticized.
- Psychological safety may support learning [S15], while surveillance deployed in its name may increase stress [S19].
- Local processing reduces exposure but does not eliminate coercion, re-identification, endpoint compromise, inference, or later misuse.
- A valid signature proves a defined cryptographic relation, not truth, authorship, consent, ownership, or authorization.

## 6. Privacy and mental-health boundary

Raw biometric, emotional, voice, facial, typing, sleep, HRV, attention, location, inferred-mood, and mental-health data should not flow to employers, managers, team-selection systems, compensation systems, or shared knowledge authorities by default.

Minimum boundary:

- Local and ephemeral processing where possible; no centralized raw-data stream.
- Strict minimization, purpose limitation, short retention, encryption, access logs, and tested deletion.
- No individual emotion, burnout, resilience, or “human spirit” score.
- No use in hiring, dismissal, promotion, pay, insurance, discipline, task assignment, or team exclusion.
- No automated diagnosis, treatment recommendation, or generic-LLM substitution for a clinician.
- No group reward linked to biometric or health disclosure.
- Treat workplace consent as power-imbalanced: opt-in, specific, time-bounded, revocable, and free from retaliation or degraded access.
- Provide worker or union governance, independent oversight, human appeal, and a non-biometric alternative.
- Apply aggregation thresholds and re-identification/leakage testing; never treat “anonymized” as risk-free.
- Say “estimates a proxy under uncertainty,” not “understands emotion” or “detects inner state.”
- Require validated instruments, representative cohorts, external validation, active controls, adverse-event monitoring, clinician escalation, and crisis procedures before health claims.
- Respect the EU workplace emotion-inference prohibition and UNESCO mental-privacy principles [S24–S25].
- Keep provenance identity non-authoritative until separate consent, access, ownership, and policy relations are proven.

## Main finding

No current standard provides a complete “agent identity plus sovereign data” contract. The defensible architecture composes separately owned layers:

```text
identifier
  -> credential/key
  -> authentication
  -> independent access-policy decision
  -> bounded data-use grant
  -> enforcement in the owning data substrate
  -> derived claim + provenance + receipt
  -> CAV2 candidate/challenge/owner-decision lifecycle
```

A valid identifier or credential supports provenance and authentication. It does not itself establish authorization, claim truth, legal consent, semantic conformance, or CyberAlchemy authority.

W3C Controlled Identifiers explicitly separates authentication from authorization [S03, §2.1.2]. VC Data Model 2.0 states that verification does not establish claim truth or fitness for reliance [S04, §§1.1, 2, 5.9]. SPIFFE identifies workloads while leaving identity meaning and authorization policy to operators [S09, §§2, 4.1].

> **Agent identity must remain provenance and authentication input unless a separate access policy authorizes the requested action. Raw sensitive data must remain in its owning substrate.**

A found standards owner is **build-from-owned**, not novelty failure. The candidate contribution is the typed composition, failure semantics, receipts, and CAV2 integration boundary—not redefinition of DID, VC, OAuth/GNAP, SPIFFE, ODRL, PROV, consent records, or transparency receipts.

| Threat | Minimum control | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|
| Correlation/cross-context collapse | Pairwise identifiers, no default equivalence links, single-audience assertions, selective disclosure | Attributes, issuer keys, status, timing and metadata can still link |

| Confused deputy | Bind client, principal, resource, audience, action and purpose | Ambient or ambiguous “on behalf of” remains dangerous |
| Over-delegation | Attenuation, one-hop default, `max_depth`, no wildcards, parent hash | Domain policy must determine whether child rights are truly narrower |
| Consent withdrawal | Separate lifecycle; deny future use; enqueue deletion duties | Does not undo past lawful processing or prove erasure |

| Model retention | Distinguish training/fine-tuning/retrieval/prompting/inference; lineage; unlearning state | Source deletion does not prove influence is absent from model |
| Revocation race | Online check for sensitive use, short-lived grants, event-time policy version | Completed processing and cached data persist |
| Receipt misuse | Sign and bind grant/policy/input/output/decision; optionally log hashes | Receipts attest issuer statements, not absence of unlogged actions |

## Failure and recovery states

- `identity_unresolved`, `authentication_failed`, `credential_expired_or_revoked`: deny; recover trust or reissue only through approved recovery; increment envelope generation and invalidate prior keys.
- `recovery_pending`: deny high-risk use and notify the subject/operator.
- `grant_absent`, `grant_ambiguous_or_overbroad`, `purpose_or_audience_mismatch`: deny; require a new explicit, narrower grant.
- `consent_withdrawn`: deny future use and enqueue retention/deletion obligations; do not silently substitute another lawful basis.
- `revocation_status_unknown`: deny sensitive operations.
- `custodian_unavailable`: do not export to a fallback substrate.
- `derived_lineage_missing`: quarantine output; recompute with complete provenance or discard.
- `deletion_pending`: block onward use. If deletion/model unlearning fails, preserve the failure receipt and do not claim erasure.
- `receipt_gap_or_log_fork`: quarantine dependent claims pending consistency evidence.
- `policy_conflict`: deny until explicit owner resolution creates a new policy version.
- `cross_context_linkage_detected`: stop identifier reuse, rotate pairwise identity, and sever optional equivalence links.
