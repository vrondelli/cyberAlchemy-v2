# Categorical Learning and DNG Semantic-Hierarchy Towers Strategy Result

## P1 Trigger, Lanes, And Subagents

P1 triggered on synthesis, context protection, isolation, and parallelism. The
two papers required different evidence disciplines and had to be related to the
earlier semantic-hierarchy bridge without letting category-theoretic vocabulary
or neural architecture vocabulary substitute for ontology semantics.

- Saunders Mac Lane explored the *Categorical Learning System* paper through a
  formal/axiomatic lane, checking whether its categories, functors,
  naturality, adjunctions, and growth claims were actually defined with laws.
- Herbert Simon explored *Dynamic Neural Graph Encoding of Inference
  Processes* through an empirical/computational lane, separating the paper's
  neural-network-parameter target from the Ponder ontology target.
- John Baez synthesized the towers and completed one bounded revision after
  Karl Popper's non-vacuity attack and Willard Van Orman Quine's definitional
  non-collapse attack.
- F. William Lawvere wrote the source-linked findings. Nicolas Bourbaki
  performed an advisory final audit and returned `ACCEPT`; the parent agent,
  which was not a working dispatch agent, issued final approval.

Explorer anti-bias combined source-corpus and methodology tension. The
dependency flow was two explorers -> synthesizer <-> two skeptics -> writer ->
advisory auditor -> parent final approver. Seven working agents completed, one
synthesis/review loop was used, and no feedback edge fired.

## Accepted Result

The papers provide a falsifiable experimental decomposition, not a ready
Ponder algorithm. An unwritten adapter could serialize a frozen Ponder
projection through an authored evaluation schedule and feed a calibrated
predicate-candidate scorer. Separately specified deterministic policies alone
could then admit typed relations, operational order, or facets. No semantic
witness or target benefit exists yet.

The bounded path is:

1. re-freeze one Ponder ontology projection;
2. create `PONDER-ONTOLOGY-EVALUATOR-MINIMAL-FIXTURES.v0`;
3. specify a finite adapter from that projection to an event sequence;
4. compare the authored schedule with a schedule counterfactual;
5. score a predeclared tuple of predicate candidates;
6. apply separate relation, direct-parent, ancestor, and facet contracts;
7. project heterogeneous operational-order and facet views; and
8. compare view components without collapsing them to one scalar.
