#### Formal/axiomatic verdict

The complete 31-item audit is in `claims/claim-ledger.md`; notation and obligations are in `notation/notation-bridge.md` and `definitions/local-definitions.md`.

No categorical claim reaches `defined-with-laws`:

- The proposal names JGS instances as objects, wiring as morphisms, a no-op instance as identity, and chaining as composition (PDF p. 1), but it does not provide typed hom-sets, an arrow-valued identity, closure, equality, or law witnesses.

- The backbone is not yet a functor because source/target categories, its arrow map, and identity/composition preservation are absent (PDF pp. 1, 5, 10–11).
- Variant swapping is not yet a natural transformation because there are no two functors, per-object components, or commuting naturality squares (PDF p. 1).
- `LLM ⊣ JGS`, `Curiosity ⊣ Training`, and `DNG-Encode ⊣ DNG-Decode` are analogy-only. None supplies typed categories/functors, a natural hom-set bijection, naturality, or triangle identities (PDF pp. 2–3). The sketched LLM/JGS unit and counit are component-shaped intuitions, not law witnesses.

- DNG generation is an underdefined learned interface, and a Category Latent Vector is not a categorical invariant. Minting remains a hypernetwork/distillation hypothesis lacking serialization invariants, objectives, checkpoints, reconstruction bounds and reproducible fidelity evidence (PDF pp. 14–17, 23–26, 32).

DNG actually processes neural-network weights, biases, and engineered forward-pass structure. A static neural graph encodes layer biases as node features and weights as edge features, with RFF representations; the DNG replaces the fixed whole-network graph with timestamped adjacent-layer bipartite snapshots, adding the next layer and deleting prior structure as it follows the neural forward pass (PDF pp.3, 5). DNG-Encoder then uses edge-conditioned elementwise messages, GRU plus LayerNorm node memories, and no edge updates because edges are used only at their designated timestamp (PDF pp.6–7). INR2JLS combines the final memory with learned spatial vectors and reconstructs the image represented by an INR—not the weights—before a frozen-encoder classifier uses that latent map (PDF pp.8–10). Exact formal objects, equations, CNN/transformer conversion, and the restricted neuron-permutation proof are in [notation bridge](cyberAlchemy-v2/development/research/2026-07-22-categorical-dng-semantic-hierarchy-towers/towers/dynamic-neural-graph-inference/notation-bridge.md).

The empirical audit distinguishes reported results from reproduced evidence. The paper’s activation-fitting study uses 1,000 train and 500 test three-layer random MLPs and reports dynamic-graph fitting remains low while static fitting degrades after the first layer (PDF p.9). Table 1 reports INR2JLS classification accuracy of 98.6/90.6/73.2/42.4% on MNIST/FashionMNIST/CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100, versus NG-T’s 96.83/85.24/57.7/31.65% (PDF p.10). Table 2 reports Kendall tau for CNN generalization prediction of 0.936, 0.867, and 0.8743 on CIFAR-10-GS, SVHN-GS, and CNN Wild Park; importantly, DNG is not best on SVHN-GS, where NG-T is 0.872 (PDF p.11). The appendical ViT experiment reports tau 0.9028 with 0.12 GFLOPs, 25.36MB, and 4.38ms under its stated benchmark (PDF p.26). These remain paper-reported, not independently rerun. [Empirical audit](cyberAlchemy-v2/development/research/2026-07-22-categorical-dng-semantic-hierarchy-towers/towers/dynamic-neural-graph-inference/empirical-audit.md) and [claim ledger](cyberAlchemy-v2/development/research/2026-07-22-categorical-dng-semantic-hierarchy-towers/towers/dynamic-neural-graph-inference/claim-ledger.md) preserve datasets, training details, baselines, metrics, ablations, limitations, and claim strength.
