# Constitution Foundry to RWO kernel integration research

Status: candidate proposal; unregistered and unexecuted. This is a private, non-authority strategy projection. `dispatch-sheet.json` owns the exact dispatch material.

## P1 trigger

Dispatch is warranted by synthesis, context protection, isolation, and parallelism. The question crosses private Foundry architecture, public orchestrator current implementation, proposal-only RWO types, formal-backend semantics, authority, Mint effects, recovery, and future conformance. A single linear reading would make it too easy to collapse structural orchestration with domain truth or to mistake the existing control plane for the proposed recursive kernel.

## Preliminary answer

The safest candidate relationship is **Foundry as a typed domain composition over the thin kernel**, not Foundry inside the kernel and not the kernel as Meta-Authority.

- `Author`, `Prove`, `Decide`, and `Cast` can be modeled as leaf or composite `Work` definitions and composed recursively.
- Foundry owns constitution cases, native-source/IR semantics, obligation aggregation, backend reports, derived case posture, and the distinction between ratification and casting authorization.
- Cedar, Lean, Datalog, and bounded solvers remain independently owned executor adapters or work units; a kernel release event is never their semantic verdict.
- Existing constitution and authority owners issue decisions. The narrow Meta-Authority interfaces remain prerequisite validation and owner-route advice; they do not become kernel authority.
- Mint remains the only target-effect and transaction/recovery owner. The kernel may deliver an authorized command through an executor adapter and observe structural events, but cannot reinterpret transaction or observation outcomes.
- The RWO journal/cursor/status projection must remain distinct from Foundry receipts and case state. One accepted event history may support replay, but it cannot manufacture domain truth.

This is still a research hypothesis. The current orchestrator has substantial implemented control-plane/evidence machinery, while the recursive Work kernel, composition-form metamodel, and ontology remain proposal/candidate surfaces with unexecuted conformance residue.

## Private/public boundary repair

The target repository requires every governed seat prompt to carry an `ACI-WORKFLOW-BINDING-V1` envelope compiled and persisted inside that repository. Using that path for this research would persist the private Foundry goal and prompts in the public repository. Skipping it would create orphaned seats.

The strategy therefore freezes a parent-observed, digest-bound public-kernel snapshot in `strategy-inventory-packet.json`. Research agents may read that private packet, the accepted parent-repository current-state findings, and private Foundry sources. They are explicitly forbidden from opening or writing `projects/cyberalchemy-orchestrator`. This keeps the dispatch under the parent repository's governed lifecycle and prevents both private leakage and wrapper bypass.

The resulting evidence ceiling is explicit: public-source facts are independently observed and hashed by the parent preflight, then consumed by agents through the packet. Agents do not claim a second direct inspection of those public bytes.

## Dependency flow

`explorers -> synthesizer <-> reviewers -> evidence closure -> writer <-> auditor`.

- Explorers run in parallel and all must join.
- The synthesizer-reviewer zig-zag has one loop.
- One feedback pass to explorers may repair exact-source or type-mapping gaps.
- The writer stays blocked until the parent records exact-binding closure as `ready`.
- The auditor may request one bounded writer revision.
- The singleton auditor is the final approver; the parent owns user communication and closeout claims.

## Required result

The final research must contain:

1. a plain-language explanation of how Foundry sits on the kernel;
2. an owner and layer map;
3. a type map classified as direct reuse, adapter, external owner, or forbidden collapse;
4. an `Author -> Prove -> Decide -> Cast` recursive Work graph;
5. a command/event/gate/effect/receipt timeline;
6. a current-implementation versus proposal-only versus missing-delta matrix;
7. a candidate `FoundryWorkAdapter` contract;
8. journal, replay, retry, repeat, recovery, and observation boundaries;
9. finite positive and negative conformance fixtures;
10. rejected alternatives and owner-routed implementation gates.

## Evidence contract

Use `exact-binding`. Every retained claim must include:

- a stable candidate or mapping ID;
- parent-observed public source path, SHA-256, and selector from `strategy-inventory-packet.json`;
- private Foundry source path and exact selector;
- explorer return reference and evidence scope;
- proof status: `observed`, `executed`, `proposal-only`, `planned`, or `unverified`;
- current owner and required adapter or non-correspondence;
- smallest positive witness and discriminating negative where applicable;
- authority, mutation, promotion, publication, deployment, release, and production ceiling.

Inventory is discovery-only. The parent source packet is evidence transport, not new authority or a replacement for the public owners.

## Stop conditions

Stop rather than infer if:

- a required public-kernel fact is absent from or contradicted by the frozen packet;
- an agent would need to open the nested public repository;
- a mapping requires the kernel to own semantic judgment, authority, confirmation, domain state, Mint effects, or ontology promotion;
- an integration claim has no finite discriminating witness;
- owner identity, authority subject, journal truth, recovery classification, or effect reference is unresolved;
- the work would mutate source, ontology, authority, Inventory, telemetry, Git, or an external system.
