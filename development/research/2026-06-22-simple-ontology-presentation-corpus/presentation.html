Core Model

Between discovery and reliance, meaning needs structure.

A claim is not ready for use until its identity, relation, evidence, commitment, and consequence are visible.

Meaning with receipts. Use with limits.

  • Identity: what kind of object this is, not just where it lives.
  • Relation: what it supports, constrains, contradicts, supersedes, or realizes.
  • Evidence: why this interpretation deserves confidence.
  • Commitment: whether a person or process has accepted it for use.
  • Consequence: what may change if someone acts on it.

Split Before Promotion

Kind

A phrase that compresses a question, not yet a settled concept.

Receipt

Confidence

Contestable

Decision Surface

Ontology points. Decisions authorize.

The map can prepare the decision surface. It cannot convert a well-supported meaning into permission to act.
  • A claim starts as a candidate, not a rule.
  • Evidence explains why to believe; it does not grant permission.
  • Accepted meaning has scope. Outside that scope, it becomes a candidate again.
  • A view can inform review; it cannot settle disputed status.
  • If acting on a claim changes behavior, cost, or risk, route it to a decision.
