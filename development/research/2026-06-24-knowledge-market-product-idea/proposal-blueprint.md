## One-Line Promise

Make the shoulders visible first: CyberAlchemy Evidence Exchange turns governed reuse of knowledge into reviewable receipts, credit seeds, and future reward eligibility without letting market signals become authority.

## Executive Brief

CyberAlchemy Evidence Exchange is a local-first exchange/workbench for reusable knowledge labor. It lets individuals, groups, projects, local AI nodes, and governed ontologies register reusable work, observe when that work is used, review the claimed relationship, and seed credit for future recognition or reward.

The product is not a generic knowledge marketplace. It is not a truth market. It is not a DAO payout layer. It is a governed evidence exchange: a place where a project can say "this artifact, ontology, research tower, domain-logic module, consulting package, or review actually helped produce that downstream artifact, and here is the receipt."

The strategic move is to separate three things that are often collapsed:

1. Reuse: a downstream work used an upstream work.
2. Credit: reviewers accepted or revised the contribution weight.
3. Authority: an owner route promoted something into relied-on CyberAlchemy authority.

The first proof should be small and sharp: one source work `W0` is retrieved through a governed local AI surface, contributes to a downstream artifact `W1`, emits a candidate `ObservedUseReceipt`, gets reviewed, and updates credit seeds while authority remains unchanged. If that witness works, the system can gradually support richer assets: research towers, made projects, specific domain logic, consulting artifacts, individual ontologies, group ontologies, review records, and promoted authority projections.

The long-term product vision is a knowledge network where local AI nodes can share, retrieve, distribute, and improve governed knowledge across groups and individuals. Tokens may eventually fund review, reward distribution, or contribution-backed project capital, but only after the credit graph is useful without money and the legal, tax, identity, wallet, governance, privacy, anti-gaming, and human approval gates exist.

## 1. The Frustration

A team finishes a strong artifact with help from old research, a consultant's domain model, a group ontology, a prior project, and a local AI node that selected the right context. The final artifact looks like one new output. The shoulders disappear.

That is bad for contributors because high-value knowledge labor becomes invisible. It is bad for projects because they cannot tell which upstream work is compounding. It is bad for governance because popularity and confidence can start masquerading as authority. It is bad for future reward systems because payout logic arrives before anyone can prove what was actually used.

What breaks:

1. Knowledge does not compound visibly. Reusable work is copied, summarized, or invoked, but the system cannot show what made the new work possible.
2. Contribution and authority collapse. A useful artifact, popular ontology, or frequently reused research note can start feeling authoritative without owner-routed promotion.
3. Rewards come too early. If tokens or payouts arrive before receipts, review, privacy, and anti-gaming controls, the product rewards performance theater instead of useful knowledge work.
4. Non-research labor stays under-modeled. Made projects, domain logic, consulting packages, ontology maintenance, challenge work, and packaging labor are often as valuable as papers, but they lack a governed credit surface.

## 2. The Idea In One Image

Think of the exchange as a receipt network for knowledge work.

```text
Upstream work
  research | project | domain logic | consulting pack | individual ontology | group ontology
    -> retrieved or invoked by governed local AI node
    -> downstream artifact
    -> observed use receipt
    -> review action
    -> credit seed
    -> optional authority handoff
    -> optional future reward adapter
```

In one sentence: **CyberAlchemy Evidence Exchange is a governed market-like workbench where knowledge earns credit by being used, reviewed, maintained, challenged, packaged, or distributed, while authority still moves only through owner routes.**

The market metaphor is useful only if it is restrained. The first product is an evidence exchange, not a cash exchange. Its native unit is the reviewed contribution receipt. Tokens come later, if they come at all.

## 3. A Day With It

A group is building a compliance workflow for a client. Their local AI node retrieves three upstream assets:

1. A Tower substrate that organizes the domain.
2. A prior project artifact with tested decision logic.
3. A group ontology that names the client's roles, controls, and evidence types.

The team saves a downstream artifact: `W1-client-compliance-routing-spec`. The host emits candidate receipts saying which upstream works were used as context, which local AI node retrieved them, which run produced the draft, and which downstream artifact digest was saved.

A reviewer opens the Evidence Exchange workbench. One edge is accepted, one is revised because the context was useful but minor, and one is deferred because the ontology included private client material that cannot be exported. The accepted and revised edges update credit seeds. Nothing is promoted to authority. Nothing is paid out. But the project can now show what it built on, and the contributors can build reputation around real downstream use.

This scene is the target MVP experience, not an implemented capability. The first proof must show one artifact-to-artifact reuse path with host-observed evidence, privacy-safe references, a review action, and a credit seed that has no authority effect.

## 5. Why Chase This

The idea is worth chasing because it turns CyberAlchemy's governance strength into a product experience. Most knowledge markets start with price, reputation, or content inventory. Evidence Exchange starts with governed reuse: who used what, through which surface, with which privacy policy, reviewed by whom, with what authority effect.

Why it is credible:

- CyberAlchemy already has an authority model that separates evidence, ontology, research, generated projections, implementation success, and repetition from authority movement.
- Inventory already gives a reusable lookup substrate for candidate evidence sets and synthesis without treating those read models as authority.
- The existing Evidence Exchange finding already converges on a narrow product thesis, core loop, credit-first model, MVP sequence, and explicit token adapter gates.
- The Tower System gives a high-value asset class: source-backed domain substrates and persona candidates that could become reusable context only after witness-gated proof.
- The Shoulders of Giants / ResonantOS proposal supplies the core contribution-lineage pattern: host-observed events, typed work edges, local storage, credit before money, and reward adapter gates.

Why it is still a bet:

- Host-observed reuse has to feel natural in a real local AI workflow rather than like manual paperwork.
- Credit allocation has to be explainable, conserved, and reviewable enough that contributors trust it before tokens exist.
- Tower assets, persona candidates, and node-to-opposable-stance behavior remain witness-gated and cannot be treated as proven product surfaces yet.
- Privacy policy and redaction must be strong enough to show lineage without leaking private research, prompts, client knowledge, memory, wallet context, or source text.
- A future token layer may introduce legal, tax, securities, money-transmission, identity, and governance complexity that can kill or radically reshape the reward path.

## 7. System Blueprint

Evidence Exchange has two ledgers and one bridge:

1. Credit ledger: records reuse, review, maintenance, packaging, challenge, distribution, and credit seeds.
2. Authority ledger: records owner-routed promotion, rejection, retirement, contradiction, or projection.
3. Handoff bridge: lets a high-credit or strongly reviewed artifact request attention from an authority route without promoting itself.

### Layer Or Workflow Stack

```text
Knowledge asset layer
  Work node
  Tower substrate candidate
  Project artifact
  Domain logic module
  Consulting package
  Individual ontology
  Group ontology
  Review or verdict record
  Promoted authority projection

Governed retrieval layer
  Local AI node
  ResonantOS-style workspace
  CyberAlchemy workbench
  Tower witness track

Evidence layer
  Host-observed event
  Candidate ObservedUseReceipt
  Privacy/export policy
  Artifact digest
  Source reference

Review layer
  Accept
  Revise
  Reject
  Defer
  Challenge
  Maintain
  Package

Credit layer
  Observed reuse credit
  Verification credit
  Maintenance credit
  Packaging credit
  Distribution credit
  Novelty seed
  Review confidence

Authority layer
  Owner route
  Promotion policy
  Definitions governance
  Constitution governance
  Discipline governance
  Decision gate

Future reward adapter layer
  Recognition
  Review sponsorship
  Validation funding
  Token eligibility
  Payout adapter
```

### Flow

```text
Register W0
  -> retrieve W0 through governed local AI node
  -> produce and save W1
  -> emit candidate ObservedUseReceipt
  -> apply privacy/export policy
  -> reviewer accepts, revises, rejects, or defers edge
  -> update credit seed
  -> optionally raise authority handoff request
  -> optionally route future reward adapter after gates
```

The product must show the credit ledger and authority ledger separately. They may cross-reference each other, but they must not merge.

## 9. Gaps And Load-Bearing Stances

These are the hinges. If one fails, it changes the build, kill, or sequence.

| Gap / stance | Why it matters | Verification witness | Current posture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First host-observed surface | The product needs real observed reuse, not self-declared attribution. | One local AI workflow retrieves `W0`, produces `W1`, and saves a receipt with privacy-safe references. | Decide and test first. |
| Minimal receipt schema | Without a small stable schema, the workbench becomes prose and cannot support review. | Candidate `ObservedUseReceipt` fixture with source work, downstream work, run, context digest, output digest, privacy policy, trust, and review status. | Proposed, not promoted. |
| Privacy and redaction policy | Lineage can leak private prompts, client material, source text, memory, wallet context, or page content. | Export fixture that shows useful lineage while hiding private content. | Blocking gate. |
| Credit conservation | Contribution scores can overclaim through ancestors, duplicates, collusion, or circular review. | Credit seed fixture with bounded downstream budget, damped ancestors, deduped paths, and conflict checks. | Required before any reward claim. |
| Review trust | AI or human reviewers can rubber-stamp, drift, collude, or overfit to plausible narratives. | Review action logs with rationale refs, challenge paths, COI checks, and confidence labels. | MVP design requirement. |
| Tower witness boundary | Tower assets are valuable only if queryable graph, persona, stance, scoring, and verdict behavior can be witnessed. | D1/D2/D3 Tower witness receipts. | Conditional, not base MVP. |
| Authority handoff boundary | A high-credit artifact will create pressure to promote it. | Handoff request that routes to owner gates and leaves authority unchanged until decision. | Non-negotiable boundary. |
| Token adapter readiness | Money changes the product's risk profile. | Explicit legal, tax, identity, wallet, funding, security, privacy, anti-gaming, governance, and human approval record. | Killed for MVP; future gate only. |

## 10. Verification Plan

| Question | Method | Pass signal | Fail signal | Next route |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Can host-observed reuse be captured without fake precision? | Build or simulate one governed local AI retrieval path from `W0` to `W1`. | Receipt has stable IDs, digests, actor/run metadata, privacy policy, trust label, and review status. | Receipt is manual attribution, unverifiable, too invasive, or too hard to create. | Task-session for Stage 0 witness, or decision-gate to choose another surface. |
| Can the user understand the product before tokens are mentioned? | Prototype the workbench around lineage, review, and credit seeds only. | User can explain "what built on what" and "what got reviewed" without payout language. | User asks first about price, tokens, ownership, or marketplace listings. | Whisper/public-product copy pass plus non-goal tightening. |
| Can credit stay separate from authority? | Create a fixture where a highly reused artifact requests authority attention. | Workbench raises an authority handoff but does not promote. | Score, use count, ontology placement, or reviewer majority changes authority status. | Authority-model guardrail review and promotion-policy lint. |
| Can privacy-safe lineage be useful? | Export a receipt view with private source text, prompts, client data, memory, and wallet context redacted. | Viewer understands the relationship without seeing protected content. | Redaction removes all value or leaks protected context. | Privacy policy design before any public or contributor view. |
| Can Tower assets earn credit only after witness proof? | Run Tower D1/D2/D3 witness design against one candidate substrate. | Tower contribution is credited only through witnessed query/persona/stance/verdict behavior. | Tower artifact earns credit by reputation or label alone. | Keep Tower outside MVP until witness track passes. |
| Can future rewards be deferred without weakening the product? | Test recognition, maintenance priority, review sponsorship, and validation funding as non-token incentives. | Contributors still see value in credit and review attention. | Users treat the system as useless without immediate payout. | Re-scope to internal governance tool or run market research before token design. |

## 11. MVP Or Stage Sequence

1. Credit-only lineage witness: prove saved artifacts can show what they built on, where they were reused, which evidence supports the edge, and which trust tier applies.
2. Evidence Exchange workbench: register work, inspect receipts, apply privacy-safe export, and let reviewers accept, revise, reject, or defer edges.
3. Review, challenge, and credit seeds: add rationale refs, confidence labels, conserved credit budgets, duplicate-path checks, reciprocal-review checks, and challenge paths.
4. Authority handoff lane: show when high-credit or strongly reviewed work deserves owner attention while routing promotion through CyberAlchemy gates.
5. Tower witness track: add Tower substrates, personas, stance formation, scoring, and verdict records only after D1/D2/D3-style witness proof.
6. Optional reward adapter gate: explore tokens, payout, reward pools, or knowledge-as-project-capital only after the graph is valuable without money and all hard gates are passed.

## 12. Risks And Non-Goals

Risks:

- Observation risk: work outside governed host surfaces cannot honestly be rewarded.
- Privacy risk: lineage can leak private material unless export policy is first-class.
- Gaming risk: self-declared edges, collusion, reciprocal review, synthetic novelty, spam, and clustered reviewers can inflate credit.
- Double-counting risk: ancestor chains can overclaim unless credit is conserved, damped, and deduped.
- Review capture risk: reviewers can drift, rubber-stamp, or overfit unless rationale, challenge, COI, and audit paths exist.
- Tower proof risk: Tower runtime behavior remains witness-gated.
- Token/legal risk: payout, tokenized reward, cross-border distribution, DAO flows, securities implications, tax, and money transmission can reshape or block the reward layer.
- Vocabulary risk: "market," "asset," "credit," "reward," "token," and "knowledge capital" can imply ownership, truth, payout, or securities unless definitions stay narrow.

Non-goals:

- No truth market, authority market, generic DAO marketplace, or Tower rebrand.
- No authority-by-score, authority-by-token, authority-by-price, authority-by-usage, authority-by-ontology, or authority-by-popularity.
- No token or payout MVP.
- No public marketplace before privacy, consent, attribution, legal, identity, and anti-gaming controls exist.
- No universal cross-app tracking.
- No raw prompt, private memory, wallet context, page content, client data, or source text export by default.
- No reward eligibility for unreviewed artifacts or spam artifacts.
- No claim that legal, tax, securities, money-transmission, wallet, identity, funding, or governance readiness already exists.
