Reader terms

Plain language for the load-bearing ideas

Each term includes why it matters and the misuse this decision must avoid.

Formation contract

The machine-checkable rules deciding whether a node body matches its kind. Why it matters: current v1 names JSON rules but executes YAML checks. The current adapter is not newly authoritative.

Current v1

The existing four-kind minted-project profile and its historical declarations. Why it matters: freezing it prevents a silent compatibility rewrite. Frozen does not mean complete.

Versioned replacement

A new profile and contract version that can give YAML rule ownership honestly. Why it matters: it exposes migration and evidence boundaries. A design document alone does not admit it.

Retirement map

An exact account of every old JSON constraint and its new destination or explicit retirement impact. Why it matters: a prose claim that JSON is retired cannot expose missing rules.

False green

A pass that skipped a governing contract. Why it matters: the known Entity case can pass YAML while violating declared JSON. Existing regression totals do not close it.

Machine admission

The owner-controlled act that makes a particular profile version usable. Why it matters: it separates an accepted direction from live behavior. This x-ray and generated receipts cannot self-admit.

required_structure

The literal tables or sections a kind must supply. Why it matters: the kernel loads it but does not evaluate it across all 13 backend schemas. Named criteria alone are insufficient.

Exact-set proof

Compare the identities of every required and actual item, not totals. Why it matters: equal counts can hide missing, extra, duplicated, stale, skipped, or surviving items.
