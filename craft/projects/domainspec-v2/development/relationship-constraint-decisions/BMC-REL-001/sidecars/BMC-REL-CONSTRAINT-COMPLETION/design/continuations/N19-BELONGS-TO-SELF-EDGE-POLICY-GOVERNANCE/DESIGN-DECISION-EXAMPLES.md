# N19 Complexity Example Ladder

All scenarios are hypothetical. The invariant is governance partitioning for one
direct canonical `belongs-to` proposition whose source and target Entity concept IDs
are equal inside one exact admitted D30 kind. No example rejects or allows that fact.
`decision_effect: none`.

## Low Complexity — One Classification Candidate

Scenario: hypothetical `CategoryA --[belongs-to_classification]--> CategoryA`.

- **A — Uniform:** route this candidate to one future self-edge rule that must also
  govern every other current or later admitted membership kind.
- **B — Kind-specific:** route this candidate to a future `classification`-only
  self-edge decision; `home-assignment` remains independently unresolved.
- **C — Defer:** leave governance shape unresolved and keep Design blocked.
- **D — Stop:** terminate this continuation without self-edge-policy authority.

Demonstrates: one shared future rule versus one kind-local future rule. Does not prove
whether the classification self-edge is valid or semantically useful.

## Medium Complexity — Two Active Kinds

Added complexity: the same hypothetical Entity has separate self-edge candidates under
`classification` and `home-assignment`.

- **A — Uniform:** both candidates must later receive the same self-edge behavior
  because one rule spans every admitted kind.
- **B — Kind-specific:** each active kind receives its own explicit future owner
  decision and independently validated behavior.
- **C — Defer:** both candidates remain blocked with no governance partition selected.
- **D — Stop:** end the route; neither candidate gains self-edge behavior.

Demonstrates: uniformity reduces policy surfaces while kind-specific governance
preserves D30 meaning separation. Does not authorize either candidate or require membership.

## Complex — Registry Evolution And Independent Residue

Added complexity: classification and home-assignment candidates occur in different
features, a two-Entity membership cycle exists separately, and a future Definitions
decision may add another active membership kind.

- **A — Uniform:** one future rule covers both current kinds and any later-added kind;
  the longer cycle and cross-feature closure remain outside that rule.
- **B — Kind-specific:** every current or later-added kind requires its own explicit
  self-edge behavior decision and registry-digest review; the longer cycle remains separate.
- **C — Defer:** registry evolution and all self-edge candidates remain blocked on
  this governance choice.
- **D — Stop:** terminate without governing current or future membership-kind self-edges.

Demonstrates: registry evolution, validation cost, and preservation of independent
topology residue. Does not prove cycle behavior, transitivity, closure, duplicates,
representation, runtime behavior, numeric bounds, or packet authority.

## Coverage

- A, B, C, and D appear exactly once per rung with equivalent scenario depth.
- Unsupported claims: 0; every scenario detail is labelled hypothetical.
- Recommendation preserved: B, kind-specific governance.
- Decision effect: none.
