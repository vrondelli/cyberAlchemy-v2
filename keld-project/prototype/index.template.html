KELD / THE ILLUSTRATED LEGEND

He was known
by many titles.

Some knew what he built.
Others only knew what he kept.

The workshop of Keld

Some knew what he built. Others only knew what he kept.

There was a workshop where abandoned mechanisms, unfinished ideas, and questions without answers were given a place on the shelves.

It carried the name of its maker.

Hoarder of Secrets

The archivists called him the Hoarder of Secrets.

Keld collected what others discarded: abandoned mechanisms, failed experiments, notebooks whose authors had crossed out the most interesting parts. He kept drawers of components for machines that had never existed.

His were not the secrets people kept from one another, but the ones the world kept from everyone.

Cyber Blacksmith

The engineers called him the Cyber Blacksmith.

Those who expected a furnace were not entirely disappointed. There was heat in the workshop, and metal, and the patient sound of something being shaped.

But there were also glass instruments, cabinets of memory, and fine copper lines disappearing beneath the workbench.

Some evenings, the machines continued working after the lights went out.

Keeper of the Unmade

Others knew him as the Keeper of the Unmade.

On the highest shelves stood unfinished instruments, each waiting for a missing piece.

Beside one lay an empty space and a handwritten label:

For a problem that has not arrived yet.

Tools for another mind

But the minds that found their way to his door had a simpler name for him.

The maker.

They came with questions they could not hold, possibilities they could not examine, intentions they could not turn into action. Some were old and learned. Others had only recently begun to think.

Keld did not offer to think in their place.

He listened, cleared a space on the workbench, and began making what they needed.

An instrument to carry a memory. A mechanism to test a possibility. A connection where there had been none.

Even minds need tools.

Every finished piece bore the same small mark: a core surrounded by carefully fitted forms.

When asked why he put so much care into things that would serve another mind, Keld gave the same answer.

“Even minds need tools.”

The workshop still carries his name.

KELD / AGENT GEAR

FEATURED WORK / 001 · ALPHA

Agent
Reasoning Engine.

A second skin for your agent.

Your way of working, connected.

A.R.E. brings language, context, memory and tools into one coherent working experience. It helps your agent understand what you mean, preserve useful progress, and adapt as the task changes.

It serves both sides of the conversation: explanations and control for you; relevant context, explicit tool contracts and feedback for your agent.

Two cycles, one engine.

The reasoning cycle connects the goal, evidence and next action. The evolution cycle uses observed weaknesses to form hypotheses, compare experiments and recommend improvements. Applying an engine change remains a separate decision.

AN ILLUSTRATIVE MODEL, NOT A LIVE ENGINE DEMO

What is the question, really?

A task arrives with context, constraints, and things that are not yet known. This is where an instrument of reasoning begins: making room for what matters.

What deserves consideration?

Possibilities are not decisions. The question is how to examine alternatives, recognize missing information, and make the next choice more deliberate.

What is the next useful step?

A considered next step might be an action, another question, or a decision to stop. These are the design questions behind the gear—not a claim about an implemented workflow.

Alpha. Downloadable. Apache licensed.

ARE is an alpha version, delivered as a downloadable software package under Apache License 2.0. Expect bugs and changes while the engine develops; test it with your own setup. This is not access to a hosted engine. The base price is $200 USD, with an editable price selector in the shop.

The first 100 customers

One year of ongoing ARE updates and upgrades as they are released, plus direct support from the maker for that same year. Ask questions, work through issues, share feedback, and discuss improvements. The period starts on purchase, and the benefits are the same at every accepted price within the first 100 customers.

There is no fixed release cadence, response-time guarantee, or commitment to build every requested feature. The actual package, version, system requirements, and support channel still need to be published before sales open. This preview does not process payments or reserve places.

KELD / FIELD NOTES

FIELD NOTE / 01 · THE IDEA

What we mean
by agent gear.

The agent is not the whole story.

When we talk about agent gear, we mean the instruments around an agent: the tools and systems that give its work structure, continuity, and a connection to the world.

The phrase is deliberately practical. Gear is something you equip for a purpose. It has a job to do. It can be understood, selected, fitted, maintained, and changed.

Equip the mind. Don’t replace its purpose.

We use the workshop as our organizing idea. A maker does not need to become the adventurer. The maker needs to understand the journey well enough to build equipment that belongs on it.

For Keld, that means beginning with questions about reasoning, memory, context, and action. These are territories for the work, not a claim that we already offer a finished product in each one.

A category with a clear center.

The Agent Reasoning Engine is the first piece of work we introduce on this site. It belongs to the category because it concerns the equipment of reasoning: the space between a question and a useful next step.

Our name for the category is an invitation to think differently about what gets built. Not only “What can this agent do?” but also “What should this agent have to work with?”

Even minds need tools.

FIELD NOTE / 02 · THE APPROACH

The maker,
not the magician.

Let the story be mysterious. Let the work be understandable.

We gave Keld a legend because making has room for imagination. A workshop full of impossible instruments is a good place to begin a question.

It is not a good place to end a technical explanation.

Wonder is an invitation.

The Cyber Blacksmith is a fictional figure. His cabinets of memory and shelves of unfinished devices give the studio a language: instruments, components, workbenches, archives.

That language should bring people closer to the work. It should never make the work harder to inspect.

The craft is in the details.

A clear interface, an honest limitation, and an example that someone can examine belong to the same idea of craft as an elegant mark. They make the instrument easier to understand.

We want to earn interest through what is built, rather than borrow credibility from a story about what might be possible.

The legend is fiction.
The work is real.

That is the line we want the site to hold: enough imagination to open a door, enough clarity to show what is actually on the other side.
KELD / FIELD NOTES

FIELD NOTE / 03 · THE MARK

A mind
at the center.

A core. A surround. The space between them.

Keld’s mark places a small hexagonal core inside three fitted forms. The surrounding pieces hold together without becoming a single closed mass.

Our interpretation is simple: intelligence at the center, instruments around it.

A signature, not a system diagram.

The mark does not describe the technical architecture of a product. Its pieces are not assigned to three hidden modules. It is a visual idea about equipment, fit, and the relationship between a mind and its tools.

In the legend, it is engraved on every finished instrument. On the site, it is a maker’s signature: a small indication of where the work comes from.

Made to be carried.

The symbol should make sense on a page, beside a product name, or by itself. It should not need the entire legend to be recognizable.

The story adds another layer for the people who choose to look closer.

Some marks identify the object.
This one identifies the workshop.
