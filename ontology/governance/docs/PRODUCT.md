# Governance Product

Status: perennial candidate. No authority or enforcement effect.
Owner: Governance product ontology owner.

In plain terms: this is a long-lived product proposal. It grants no permission
to govern work.

## Purpose

Governance turns principles, invariants, policies, and owner decisions into
explicit, scoped, challengeable, and operable controls for work, artifacts,
agents, and systems. It also makes governance itself observable and
improvable.

The product serves two responsibilities without collapsing them:

1. describe, evaluate, decide, observe, and evolve governance without effects;
   and
2. validate or enforce an exact adopted and applicable control through a
   separately owned authority and runtime binding.

Success means necessary protections remain effective, authority is legible,
conflicting or missing governance is visible, unjustified burden is
challengeable, and consequential actions remain controlled and explainable.
Standalone packaging, lifecycle choreography, hashing, and one runtime are
implementation concerns inside that product, not its purpose.

## Product Thesis

Governance should be evidence-grounded, authority-bounded, operable,
proportionate, and capable of learning. Its concepts must preserve the
differences among meaning, evidence, applicability, evaluation, decision,
adoption, authority, validation, enforcement, observation, and improvement.

Governance is not one mandatory linear pipeline. A Constitution may compose
rules; a discipline may organize recurring practice; a decision may defer; an
observer may report without deciding; an evaluation may be `not_evaluable`;
and an adopted rule may remain unenforced until a valid binding exists.

## Actors And Work

The starting actors are:

- **Governance Steward** — maintains a bounded governance portfolio and routes
  conflicts or improvement proposals to responsible owners.
- **Decision Owner** — makes one exact governance decision inside an explicit
  authority envelope.
- **Governed Participant** — a person, agent, tool, or system whose proposed
  work may match applicable governance.
- **Independent Observer** — records and explains governance behavior without
  receiving decision or execution authority.
- **Validator Or Enforcer** — evaluates a declared binding and returns an exact
  result without inventing governance or authority.

The work domain is **Governed Work And Systems**. It includes artifacts,
processes, agents, capabilities, repositories, and systems where correctness,
integrity, privacy, safety, authority, coordination, or consequence warrants
explicit governance.

Participation is not authority. Representation in the ontology does not make
every rule applicable to every subject.

## Problems

The product addresses five enduring problem families:

1. **Implicit Or Non-Operable Governance** — expectations exist only in prose,
   convention, or memory and cannot be selected, evaluated, or run.
2. **Authority Ambiguity And Leakage** — ownership, evidence, validation, or
   proximity is mistaken for permission to decide or cause an effect.
3. **Fragmented And Inconsistent Governance** — rules, policies,
   Constitutions, disciplines, gates, and exceptions conflict or compose
   without visible precedence.
4. **Unjustified Or Unevaluated Governance** — controls accumulate without a
   named protection, risk basis, owner, effectiveness evidence, or review.
5. **Governance Attenuation And Blind Spots** — evidence is missing,
   observation is incomplete, or governance weakens across translation and
   execution boundaries.

Lifecycle churn is one observed symptom of governance friction within these
families; it is not a sixth product center.

## Capabilities

The product has seven enduring capabilities:

1. **Define And Compose Governance** — express axioms, invariants, governance
   rules, policies, Constitutions, disciplines, contracts, scope, precedence,
   and conflict behavior.
2. **Route Authority And Decisions** — expose the responsible owner, decision
   kind, authority ceiling, admissible choices, delegation, deferral,
   exception, override, and escalation.
3. **Evaluate Governance** — assess justification, applicability, coherence,
   proportionality, observability, and executable fitness.
4. **Validate And Enforce Applicable Governance** — execute only declared
   adopted bindings and preserve allow, deny, and `not_evaluable` outcomes.
5. **Observe, Audit, And Explain Governance** — preserve evidence, findings,
   missing-evidence states, causal explanations, privacy boundaries, and claim
   ceilings.
6. **Evaluate And Evolve Governance** — propose, review, adopt, correct,
   attenuate, supersede, or retire governance through evidence and responsible
   owner decisions.
7. **Operate Governance** — manage ownership, inventory, versions, coverage,
   conflicts, health, cross-capability consistency, and bounded exceptions.

Task Session and Governance Flow are application and optimization subdomains
that can use these capabilities. They do not define the complete product.

## Outputs And Outcomes

The product produces five output families:

- a **Governance Contract** describing selected meaning, scope, applicability,
  authority dependencies, and evaluation behavior;
- a **Governance Decision Record** preserving one exact owner choice and its
  limits;
- a **Governance Evaluation** recording evidence, gaps, fitness, and a bounded
  recommendation or `not_evaluable` status;
- a **Governance Enforcement Result** recording the exact binding, inputs,
  verdict, evidence, and claim ceiling; and
- a **Governance Evidence And Health View** making coverage, effectiveness,
  friction, drift, missing evidence, and learning visible.

The intended outcomes are **Accountable Governance**, **Controlled
Consequences**, and **Proportionate And Adaptive Governance**. None is proved
by the existence or validation of this candidate.

## Business Rules

The product ontology declares nine candidate rules:

1. **Governance Must Name Its Basis.** A control must identify the invariant,
   harm, risk, obligation, or authority boundary it protects. Missing evidence
   narrows the claim or yields `not_evaluable`; it does not automatically
   remove the control.
2. **Authority Must Be Bounded And Visible.** Holder, action, target, scope,
   conditions, validity, and effect ceiling must not be inferred.
3. **Only Adopted Applicable Governance May Enforce.** Presence, candidacy,
   evidence, validation, or selection is not enforcement eligibility.
4. **Evidence, Decision, And Effect Must Stay Separate.** Observation,
   evaluation, recommendation, owner choice, adoption, and execution are
   distinct acts.
5. **Critical Governance Must Be Runnable.** A claimed executable control
   needs a deterministic validation or enforcement contract and explicit
   missing-input behavior.
6. **Observation Must Remain Independent From Execution.** An observer may
   report evidence and gaps but cannot infer a verdict, owner decision, or
   effect.
7. **Governance Serves The Work.** Burden must be proportionate and routine
   mechanics should be carried by the system rather than repeatedly exported
   to participants.
8. **Integrity Evidence Must Not Become Semantic Authority.** Hashes can prove
   selected byte equality, but cannot create meaning, adoption, authority, or
   lifecycle movement.
9. **Governance Evolves Through Evidence And Owner Decisions.** Correction,
   exception, supersession, and retirement require explicit provenance and an
   authorized owner route.

These rules describe candidate product semantics. They are not an adopted
Governance Constitution.

## Value And Measures

The product uses six complementary measurement families:

- **Governance Coverage** — whether consequential subjects and controls have
  explicit ownership, applicability, and evaluation paths;
- **Governance Control Effectiveness** — whether controls detect or prevent
  the named failure without unacceptable false results;
- **Authority Legibility** — whether responsible actors can identify who may
  decide or act and under which limits;
- **Governance Friction** — time, interruptions, duplicated work, handoffs,
  exceptions, and cognitive or coordination burden caused by governance;
- **Governance Refresh Churn** — repeated currentness or lifecycle refresh work
  per bounded objective, excluding semantic changes and owner decisions; and
- **Governance Learning Effectiveness** — whether observations become owned,
  evidence-backed improvements without erasing protections.

No baseline, event population, target, or KPI is adopted. Friction measures
must be paired with protection and control-effectiveness measures so burden
cannot be reduced by silently removing necessary governance.

## Boundaries

The product does not:

- redefine canonical CAV2, DomainSpec, or public Arcanum terms;
- appoint owners, adopt a rule, grant authority, or infer applicability;
- turn a Constitution, discipline, policy, observation, or passing validator
  into an effect;
- attach gates or validators to repository work;
- make Task Session or Governance Flow the universal governance lifecycle;
- treat hashes as semantic or lifecycle clocks;
- implement or prove the Rust enforcement runtime;
- extract or publish the private module; or
- authorize release, deployment, destructive action, or external effect.

The standalone module is an architecture and ownership boundary for developing
the product. It is not the product's purpose or outcome.

## Example

Example only: no real rule or publication action is changed.

A team wants to govern publication. A named invariant supplies the protection
basis. A Constitution composes the applicable rule and policy for an exact
publication scope. A Decision Owner adopts that version inside an explicit
authority envelope. A validator checks a proposed publication through a
declared binding and returns `deny` when exact authorization is absent. An
Independent Observer records the result and missing evidence without deciding
what happens next. Repeated false blocks may later support an evaluation and
an owner-routed improvement proposal.

Each artifact and act remains distinct. Hashes may identify the exact evaluated
bytes but cannot adopt the rule, authorize publication, or change its
lifecycle.

## Owners

- The **Governance product ontology owner** owns candidate product meaning.
- A **Governance Steward** owns a declared portfolio, inventory, and routing
  boundary.
- The **source governance owner** owns a rule, policy, Constitution,
  discipline, or contract in its canonical context.
- The **Decision Owner** owns one exact decision inside a valid authority
  envelope.
- The **Independent Observer** owns observation provenance and claim ceilings,
  not decisions.
- The **Validator Or Enforcer owner** owns executable bindings and result
  integrity, not source meaning.
- The **future runtime owner** owns implementation and runtime contracts.
- The **CyberAlchemy v2 authority owner** owns canonical authority meaning.

A document may describe these roles but cannot appoint them.

## Claim Ceiling

This document explains the candidate Governance product. It does not establish
measured value, canonical admission, rule fitness, owner acceptance,
Constitution adoption, runtime implementation, enforcement, publication,
release, deployment, or external effect.
