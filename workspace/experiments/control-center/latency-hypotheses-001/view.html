Prospective latency alternatives

Timing inspection → four untested hypotheses. No experiment registered or executed.

frame-wait

Frame scheduling may account for more of the handshake measurement than the status IPC round trip.

Mechanism: The existing interval ends after a requestAnimationFrame callback, so main-thread scheduling contributes to its duration.

Falsifier: In a qualified paired campaign, post-status frame wait is no greater than status IPC in most sessions and the controlled scheduling change does not reduce it.

Controlled change: Same instrumentation and status semantics; defer only identified unrelated startup UI work until after the callback.

Required spans: frontend status invoke round trip; frontend status response to frame callback

startup-phases

Late prewarm completion or upstream initialization may explain startup delay even when chat_connect itself is short.

Mechanism: Native prewarm, process acquisition, bridge startup, initialize and thread/start are distinct intervals; process presence does not imply readiness.

Falsifier: Correlated qualified sessions show readiness already emitted before binding, with neither prewarm waiting nor upstream initialization on the launch-to-ready critical path; a scheduling-only prewarm change does not improve that path.

Controlled change: Move only the prewarm scheduling point earlier without reusing a process from a previous desktop session or changing initialization semantics.

Required spans: native prewarm start/end and outcome; native acquisition and mutex wait; Node bridge entry to session.ready; Node initialize round trip; Node thread/start round trip; frontend monotonic connect to ready receive

ready-replay

Buffered session.ready delivery may dominate the observed connect-to-ready interval after upstream initialization has finished.

Mechanism: Event binding and replay determine when a previously emitted ready event reaches the frontend.

Falsifier: Correlated qualified traces show ready is never buffered or replay residence is negligible, and changing replay scheduling alone does not reduce visible-ready latency.

Controlled change: Prioritize replay immediately after binding, preserving event ordering, deduplication and delivery semantics.

Required spans: native buffer enqueue to dequeue; frontend replay invoke round trip; ready event correlation ID and replay flag

projection-scroll

Frontend batching and conditional scrolling may explain delta-to-frame delay independently of upstream delta production.

Mechanism: The oldest batched delta anchors the metric, projection is timestamped before scrolling, and paint ends at a frame callback rather than presented pixels.

Falsifier: Qualified correlated spans show batching, DOM update and scrolling are small relative to delivery, and coalescing redundant scroll operations does not improve receive-to-frame timing.

Controlled change: Coalesce redundant scroll operations within each existing batch while preserving text order and user scroll intent.

Required spans: frontend receive to flush; frontend DOM update; frontend conditional scroll; frontend projection to frame callback
