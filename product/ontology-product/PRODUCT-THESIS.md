## Product Identity

The **CyberAlchemy Ontology Product** (`CA2-PRODUCT-ONTOLOGY`) is a subproduct
of **CyberAlchemy** (`CA2-PRODUCT-CYBERALCHEMY`). It applies CyberAlchemy's
human-agency, evidence-continuity, owner-continuity, honest-incompleteness, and
bounded-authority commitments to the specific work of building and using
project-owned ontologies.

The subproduct relationship does not make every CyberAlchemy artifact part of
the Ontology Product. It identifies the parent product whose principles the
subproduct must preserve.

## Thesis

Projects should be able to turn owner-selected source meaning into a durable,
typed, source-bound ontology that people and machines can inspect, validate,
query, evolve, and reuse without surrendering meaning ownership to a central
tool or mistaking an ontology projection for authority.

In simpler language:

> The Ontology Product helps a project keep its meaning connected, explicit,
> testable, and usable while the project remains the owner of that meaning.

This is a draft product proposition. It is not proof that the product works,
an implemented runtime, a promoted specification, or a claim that an existing
ontology package is currently valid.

## Problem

Meaning is commonly scattered across definitions, specifications, plans,
source code, tests, receipts, research, and conversations. An index can make
those artifacts findable without explaining how their concepts relate. A
one-off graph can explain one moment without preserving identity, provenance,
contradictions, open questions, or a reliable evolution path.

The resulting failure is not merely poor search. A project cannot confidently
answer which meaning is current, which source owns it, how concepts relate,
what remains unresolved, or whether a tool has silently inflated evidence into
authority.

## Participants

- **Human Participant** supplies intent and consequential judgment.
- **Work Owner** owns the relevant meaning, source, decision, or next action.
- **Machine Collaborator** maps, validates, queries, compares, and projects
  meaning inside an explicit contract.
- **Ontology Steward** maintains package identity, source bindings, relation
  rules, validation, and visible residue without becoming the meaning owner.

One person may hold several roles, but source ownership, ontology stewardship,
evidence production, and decision authority remain distinct.

## Value Mechanism

```text
owner-selected sources
  -> exact source identities and scopes
  -> typed nodes and relations
  -> explicit confidence, posture, and residue
  -> deterministic package validation
  -> bounded human and machine retrieval
  -> evidence for the next owner decision
```

The product creates value by preserving continuity across that chain. It does
not create value by accumulating the largest graph or by centralizing all
project meaning in one runtime.

## First-Order Principles

1. **Project-owned meaning** — the ontology represents source meaning but does
   not take ownership of it.
2. **Source-first traceability** — load-bearing claims retain exact source and
   selector evidence.
3. **Typed relational meaning** — useful ontology records both concepts and
   the typed relations that make them usable.
4. **Stable bounded identity** — package, node, relation, source, receipt, and
   query identities remain explicit and collision-checked.
5. **Evidence does not become authority** — validation, confidence, and runtime
   evidence cannot promote their own claims.
6. **Honest residue** — contradiction, drift, absence, uncertainty, and
   deferred work remain visible.
7. **Deterministic validation** — equal admitted bytes produce equal material
   identity and stable validation results.
8. **Bounded retrieval** — consumers use logical identities and declared views,
   not arbitrary filesystem reach.
9. **Replaceable realization** — adapters and runtimes serve the product
   contract; no one tool becomes the owner of ontology meaning.

## Intended Outcomes

- **Meaning continuity** — a later person or machine can recover the same
  bounded concepts, relations, sources, and residue.
- **Inspectability** — a consumer can explain why a record exists and what it
  may and may not support.
- **Project ownership** — the source project can evolve or replace the runtime
  without losing its ontology identity or authority boundaries.
- **Reviewable change** — drift and candidate changes become explicit inputs to
  an owner route rather than silent mutations.

These are intended outcomes, not currently validated effectiveness claims.

## Project

`CA2-PROJECT-ONTOLOGY-PRODUCT` coordinates the bounded work needed to define,
validate, and test one CyberAlchemy Ontology Product foundation. The Project
keeps the thesis, Goal, evidence, residue, design, and later runtime work under
one stable identity. It does not grant implementation or publication authority.

## Goal

`CA2-GOAL-ONTOLOGY-PRODUCT-FOUNDATION` seeks one validated private product
thesis and business-domain ontology package whose Product, Thesis, Project, and
Goal identities close under deterministic validation and preserve the
`subproduct_of` relationship to CyberAlchemy.

The Goal is not achieved by writing these candidate files. Achievement requires
independent validation evidence, owner review, and an explicit lifecycle
decision. Runtime implementation and replacement of Arcanum dependencies are
outside this Goal.

## Boundaries

The Ontology Product does not:

- invent or promote source meaning;
- replace definitions, specifications, or their owners;
- treat Inventory or search output as ontology authority;
- infer semantic equivalence from similar names or shapes;
- prove runtime behavior from a validated package;
- make Project or Goal progress equivalent to achievement;
- authorize implementation, publication, release, deployment, or successor
  work; or
- require DomainSpec v2, Arcanum Ontology Vault, or one particular runtime as
  its product identity.

## Hypotheses And Evidence

The draft thesis assumes that stable typed meaning, exact source bindings,
visible residue, deterministic validation, and bounded retrieval reduce the
human cost of reconstructing project meaning. It also assumes that a
project-owned contract can make ontology runtimes replaceable.

These hypotheses require controlled product evidence. A DomainSpec v2 package
may become the first bounded consumer witness after its byte epoch and owner
route settle. One successful package or runtime cannot prove the whole thesis.

## Revision Criteria

Revise the realization—and, if the failure is systemic, this thesis—when:

- projects cannot tell which source owns a mapped claim;
- stable identities or deterministic validation create more maintenance burden
  than the continuity they preserve;
- the graph hides rather than exposes contradictions and missing evidence;
- useful retrieval requires unbounded filesystem or source access;
- the runtime becomes a central authority that projects cannot replace; or
- a materially simpler approach preserves the same meaning, evidence, residue,
  and ownership guarantees.

## Claim Ceiling

This candidate establishes a draft product proposition and the selected
subproduct, Project, and Goal identities. It does not establish product value,
runtime completion, Arcanum replacement, publication, release, deployment, or
production readiness.
